🔑 Key Language

Quantities & Precision

Key words and phrases for reporting numbers, gaps, and approximations at work.

📝 Key vocabulary
ballpark figure — an approximate estimate, not necessarily exact.
"I don't have the exact number — can I give you a ballpark figure for now?"
shortfall — the gap between what was expected/needed and what was actually achieved.
"There's a shortfall of 15% — we didn't hit the target."
discrepancy — a difference or inconsistency between two sets of information.
"There's a discrepancy between the two reports — we need to find out why."
to account for — to explain or justify a figure, result, or difference.
"Can you account for the shortfall before the next meeting?"
to double-check — to check something a second time to make sure it's correct.
"Let me double-check the number of attendees."
🚀 Going further
to flag — to draw attention to something, often a problem.
"I'd like to flag an issue with the Q3 projections."
give or take — approximately, allowing for a small margin of error.
"It'll take three weeks, give or take a few days."
to cross-reference — to compare information from two sources to verify accuracy.
"We should cross-reference these numbers against last year's report."

📐 Grammar

Few / Little vs. A Few / A Little

The article (a/an) completely changes the meaning — from negative to positive.

⚠️ few / little — not enough

Negative meaning — less than expected, almost none.

There are few options left. (not enough to choose from)
There is little support for this idea.

Tone: problem, concern, not ideal

✅ a few / a little — some, enough

Positive meaning — some, at least something.

There are a few options we can explore.
We have a little budget remaining.

Tone: neutral or optimistic
Also remember: use few / a few with countable nouns (meetings, options, people). Use little / a little with uncountable nouns (time, budget, evidence, support).

📐 Grammar

"All" as a Pronoun

"All" can mean two slightly different things, depending on the context.

all = everything

"I hope all goes well in the presentation."
→ everything goes well

all = the only thing

"All we can do is prepare thoroughly."
→ the only thing we can do

From Argument to Action: How to Pitch with Confidence

A strong business pitch follows a clear and logical structure. First, you identify the problem — make the audience feel its impact. Then you present your solution, explaining precisely how it resolves the issue. Next, you support your claims with evidence: data, case studies, or testimonials. Finally, you end with a call to action — a specific, direct request for what you want the audience to do next. Without this structure, even the best ideas can fail to land.

One of the most powerful tools in any pitch is the comparative. Rather than simply saying "our solution is fast," the effective presenter says: "Our solution is 40% faster than the current system and costs significantly less than hiring additional staff." Comparatives help the audience see not just what your solution does, but why it is better than the alternatives they already know.

Objections are inevitable, and the best pitches address them head-on. Rather than hoping no one raises a concern, skilled presenters anticipate the most likely doubts and respond to them pre-emptively. A phrase such as "I understand the cost may seem high — let me show you the return on investment" turns a potential weakness into a demonstration of confidence and preparation.

Every pitch must end with a clear call to action. This is your moment to make a specific, concrete request: "I'd like you to approve a budget of €5,000" or "All I'm asking for today is thirty minutes of your time next week." Vague endings — "so, let us know what you think" — leave the audience with no clear next step. A strong close makes the decision easy.

Part 4
Grammar
Comparatives & Superlatives
1 Study the key language, then do the practice exercise below.
🔷 Comparatives — adjective + -er than / more + adjective + than
short adj Our solution is faster than the current system.
➜ One syllable (or two ending in -y): add -er than.
long adj This approach is more cost-effective than hiring additional staff.
➜ Two or more syllables: use more + adjective + than.
🔶 Superlatives — the + adjective + -est / the most + adjective
short adj This is the fastest method available.
long adj This is the most efficient system on the market.
➜ Superlatives describe the extreme — the best, worst, highest, most efficient of a group.
🔸 Useful patterns in pitching
intensifiers significantly more efficient  ·  far less expensive  ·  twice as fast as
emphasis by far the best option available  ·  considerably more reliable
➜ Combining intensifiers with comparatives makes your pitch more persuasive.
2 Choose the correct option to complete each sentence.
1. Our app loads three times ___ the competitor's version.
✅ faster than — "fast" is a one-syllable adjective, so we add -er than. "More fast" is incorrect; "fastest than" is not a valid form.
2. This is ___ pricing model we have ever offered to a client.
✅ the most competitive — "ever offered" signals we are comparing within a group (all our pricing models), so we need the superlative with the.
3. The new system is ___ expensive ___ the one we replaced last year.
✅ far less / than — "less" is used with adjectives in comparatives (less expensive). "Fewer" is used with countable nouns. Comparisons use "than", not "that".
4. Customer satisfaction is ___ it has ever been in the company's history.
✅ higher than — this is a comparative structure ("higher than it has ever been"). "More high" is incorrect; "the higher" is not a superlative form.

Part 5
Speaking
3 Pitch Scenarios
Situation 1 of 3
Situation 1 · The 60-second problem statement
Pitch a problem your team is currently facing that needs a budget solution. In 60 seconds, make the audience feel the impact of the problem — use numbers, real examples, or a brief story. Do not offer the solution yet.
💡 Use: "Currently, our team spends X hours per week on..." / "This is costing us approximately..." / "The impact on our team is..."
"Right now, our team spends approximately eight hours per week on manual data entry — that's the equivalent of one full working day, every week, per person. This is not just inefficient; it's creating errors. Last quarter alone, we identified twelve data inconsistencies that required correction, costing us three days of a senior analyst's time. We are essentially paying skilled people to do work that a system could handle in minutes. This is the problem I want to talk about today."
Situation 2 · The solution slide
Present your proposed solution to the problem from Situation 1. Use comparatives to show it is better than the current alternative. Make it clear, specific, and persuasive — and include at least one piece of evidence.
💡 Use: "Our solution is significantly more... than..." / "The data clearly shows..." / "Compared to the current system..."
"The solution I'm proposing is an automated data management system. It is significantly faster than our current manual process — tests show it processes data in under three minutes, compared to the eight hours we currently spend. It is also more cost-effective than hiring additional staff: the annual licence costs €4,000 — far less than the cost of one extra hire. The data from our pilot in Q3 clearly shows a 90% reduction in data errors. The value proposition is clear: we save time, reduce errors, and cut costs."
Situation 3 · Handling a tough question
After your pitch, a decision-maker asks: "Why should we trust this data? It sounds too good to be true." Respond confidently, acknowledging their concern and strengthening your credibility.
💡 Use: "I understand your concern..." / "The data comes from..." / "I'd be happy to share the full report..."
"That's a completely fair question — and I'd be concerned too if someone presented numbers like that without context. The data comes from our own internal pilot, which ran for six weeks across two departments. It was independently reviewed by our IT team. I have the full report here, and I'd be happy to share it with you after this meeting so you can review the methodology. What I can say is that the 90% error reduction figure has been consistent across all three test cycles."
💡 Useful pitching phrases
"The problem we're facing is..." — opening the argument
"Our solution is significantly more... than..." — using comparatives
"The data clearly shows..." — backing up with evidence
"I understand your concern — what I can tell you is..." — addressing objections
"What I'm asking for today is..." — the call to action

Dragon's Den
1 Prepare a 90-second pitch and face the investors!
🐉 Dragon's Den

Prepare a 90-second pitch for any product or idea — real or invented. You need to cover: the problem, your solution, why it is better than alternatives (use a comparative!), your key evidence, and your call to action.

The rest of the class plays investors (the "Dragons") and must ask at least two tough questions. After the Q&A, the Dragons vote: invest or not invest?

Think about: a problem people recognise · a solution that's clearly better · data or evidence · a clear ask

Problem: "Every day, millions of people throw away food because they forget what's in their fridge. In Europe alone, €143 billion of food is wasted every year."

Solution: "FridgeAI is a smart app that scans your fridge using your phone's camera, tracks expiry dates, and suggests recipes using what you already have."

Comparative: "It is significantly more effective than paper lists or reminder notes — it updates in real time and learns your habits."

Evidence: "In our beta test with 500 users, households reduced food waste by an average of 34% in the first month."

Call to action: "Today I'm asking for €50,000 for 10% equity to launch our marketing campaign and scale the app to 100,000 users."
